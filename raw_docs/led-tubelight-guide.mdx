  - question: "Should I look at watts or lumens?"
    answer: "Lumens. Watts tell you how much electricity the tube uses, while lumens tell you how much light you get. A good 20 W, 4 ft LED tube should give roughly 2,000 lumens (about 100 lumens per watt). A tube that claims 20 W but gives 1,400 lumens is simply a weaker product."
  - question: "Can I put an LED tube into my old tubelight fitting?"
    answer: "Often yes, but you must check which type you bought. Some LED tubes work with the old ballast (choke), while many need the ballast removed or bypassed so that mains power goes straight to the tube. Wrong wiring causes buzzing, no light, overheating, or an electric shock risk when changing the tube. Have an electrician do it, and switch off at the MCB first."
  - question: "Which colour temperature is best: 3000K, 4000K or 6500K?"

    answer: "Common causes are a poor-quality driver, a dimmer or regulator that is not compatible, a loose connection, or an old ballast that is still in the circuit. Flicker that is visible on a phone camera in slow motion usually points to a weak driver."

## What it is: The Light That Runs Every Evening

Tubelights are the main room lighting in many Indian homes. Replacing an old fluorescent tube with an LED one is one of the cheapest efficiency upgrades you can make.

```
Supply ──► Switch ──► [Driver / Ballast] ──► LED chips ──► Light
```

Three common formats:
- **Retrofit LED tube (G13, 4 ft):** replaces a fluorescent tube in an existing fitting.
- **LED batten:** a slim fitting with the driver built in. Best for new installs.
- **LED panel:** flat light for false ceilings.

---

## How it works: Why the Driver Matters Most

LEDs run on low-voltage DC. The **driver** converts the 230 V AC supply into the correct DC current.

- A good driver gives steady light, low flicker and a long life.
- A cheap driver is the usual reason an LED tube fails early, flickers, or runs hot.
- Many failures are not the LED chips but the driver.

## Specs that matter: Decoding the Label

| Specification Parameter | What This Spec Actually Is | Recommended Indian Home Spec | Why It Matters & What Happens If Ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luminous Flux (Lumens)** | The total quantity of visible light energy emitted in all directions by the LED lamp (measured in Lumens, lm), as opposed to Wattage (W), which measures only electrical power consumed. | **$\ge 2,000$ to $2,400\text{ Lumens}$** for a standard 4-foot (20W–22W) domestic batten | Many homeowners mistakenly buy tubes by "Watts", assuming a 20W tube is always bright. Substandard manufacturers use low-grade chips that draw 20W of electricity but produce only 1,300 to 1,500 lumens. You end up paying the full electric bill for dim, gloomy illumination that causes chronic eye strain. Always check the lumen number printed on the packaging—not just watts. |
| **Luminous Efficacy (lm/W)** | The true energy efficiency rating of the light fixture, calculated as total lumen output divided by electrical wattage input: $\text{Efficacy} = \frac{\text{Lumens}}{\text{Watts}}$. | **$\ge 100$ to $120\text{ lm/W}$** (Grade A efficiency benchmark under BEE Star Labelling) | Higher efficacy means more light with less heat generation. Low-grade LED battens ($< 80\text{ lm/W}$) convert more than 70% of electricity into heat inside the plastic housing, degrading the phosphorus layer, causing rapid lumen depreciation (light fading by 40% in just 6 months), and yellowing the clear polycarbonate diffuser. |
| **Colour Temperature (CCT)** | The chromatic warmth or coolness of the white light emitted by the LED chips, measured on the absolute Kelvin (K) temperature scale. | **3000K (Warm White)** for bedrooms; **4000K (Neutral White)** for kitchens, bathrooms & study; **6500K (Cool Daylight)** only for stores/utility | Retailers in India routinely push 6500K (harsh blue-white light) because it is mass-produced cheaply. Exposure to intense 6500K blue-spectrum light at night suppresses human melatonin production, disrupting sleep patterns and causing headaches. 3000K creates a relaxing, restful atmosphere in bedrooms, while 4000K provides crisp, natural, glare-free light ideal for cooking and reading. |
| **Colour Rendering Index (CRI / Ra)** | A quantitative measure (from 0 to 100) of how faithfully the light source reveals the true, natural colours of objects and skin tones compared to natural sunlight ($100\text{ CRI}$). | **$\text{CRI} \ge 80$ to $85$** (mandatory under IS 16102) | Cheap commercial-grade LED tubes cut costs with low-CRI ($< 70$) chips. Under low CRI light, vegetables and cooked food look dull and greyish, clothes appear washed out, skin tones look sickly and pale, and reading text causes visual fatigue. High CRI ($\ge 80$) renders natural skin tones and vibrant domestic interiors accurately. |
| **Driver Topology & Surge Protection** | The electronic architecture of the internal AC-DC converter—whether it uses a galvanically isolated transformer with an MOV surge suppressor vs a cheap non-isolated capacitive dropper circuit. | **Galvanically Isolated Constant-Current Driver with $\ge 2.5\text{kV}$ to $4.0\text{kV}$ Surge Protection** | Indian domestic supply lines experience frequent high-voltage inductive spikes when water pumps or elevators switch off. A cheap non-isolated driver uses a budget resistor that pops on the first voltage transient, destroying the tube within weeks. Furthermore, non-isolated drivers keep live 230V AC potential present across the LED aluminium PCB; if moisture enters or you touch the end caps during cleaning, you can receive an electric shock. Isolated drivers withstand 4kV surges and completely isolate high voltage from the lamp body. |
| **Power Factor (PF) & Total Harmonic Distortion (THD)** | The ratio of real electrical power to apparent power, and the percentage of harmonic current waveform distortion injected back into your home wiring. | **$\text{Power Factor} \ge 0.90$** and **$\text{THD} < 20\%$** (mandatory under IS 16102-2) | Low-cost unbranded battens have abysmal power factors ($0.4$ to $0.5$) and high harmonic distortion ($> 50\%$). When 10 to 15 such cheap LED lights are turned on simultaneously in a home, the distorted current causes neutral wires to overheat inside conduits, generates audible buzzing in home theater systems, and trips sensitive residual current devices (RCCBs). A PF $\ge 0.90$ guarantees clean, energy-efficient power draw. |
| **BIS Compulsory Registration (CRS)** | Mandatory statutory safety certification under the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Compulsory Registration Scheme (IS 16102 Part 1 for safety and Part 2 for performance). | Official **BIS CRS logo with valid Registration Number (R-XXXXXXXX)** printed on the tube body | Substandard LED imports without valid BIS R-numbers use flammable recycled plastics that fail glow-wire ignition tests and lack basic creepage/clearance insulation distances, posing immediate fire and shock hazards inside false ceilings. |

## Ratings & Sizing: How Much Light Does a Room Need?

A simple rule of thumb is to count lumens per square foot of floor area:

| Room | Lumens per sq ft |
|---|---|
| Bedroom, living room | About 10 to 20 |
| Kitchen | About 30 to 40 |
| Study / work area | About 40 to 50 |

**Example:** a 12 × 10 ft bedroom is 120 sq ft. At about 15 lm per sq ft, you need about 1,800 lumens, so one good 20 W tube is enough.

### What does the switch save? (illustrative)
An old 40 W fluorescent tube with a choke uses about 50 W in total. A 20 W LED tube saves around 30 W. At 6 hours a day and about ₹6 per unit:
- 30 W × 6 h × 30 days ≈ 5.4 units a month, roughly ₹32 per tube per month.
- A tube costs roughly ₹150 to ₹400, so it often pays back within a year. Use the calculator for your own numbers.

## 5 Checks Before You Buy

### Check 1: Verify the BIS registration
Use the **BIS CARE** app to look up the registration number printed beside the ISI mark. It should show the brand and the product category.

### Check 2: Compare lumens, not just watts
Look for the lumen figure on the pack. If it is missing, treat that as a warning sign.

### Check 3: Measure it
Plug the fitting into a plug-in energy meter or smart plug with power monitoring. The watts shown should be close to the label, within about 10 per cent.

### Check 4: Slow-motion flicker test
Film the lit tube with your phone camera in slow motion. Visible banding or pulsing means the driver is weak.

### Check 5: Heat test after 30 minutes
Switch the light off at the MCB before touching it. A driver or body that is too hot to hold points to poor design.

## How to Choose & What to Buy: Checklist for Homeowners

- [ ] **Lumens** matched to the room, not just a wattage.
- [ ] **Colour temperature:** 3000K for relaxing rooms, 4000K for working rooms.
- [ ] **CRI 80 or more**, especially in kitchens.
- [ ] **Battens** for new installations, and retrofit tubes only if the fitting is wired correctly.
- [ ] **BIS registration** and a valid licence number.
- [ ] **Warranty** of at least 1 year, with an invoice kept.
- [ ] **Commonly available brands in India:** Philips, Syska, Havells, Wipro, Crompton, Orient, Bajaj, Ledvance. Compare current models, since specs change often.

## 3 Common LED Tube Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Buying by watts only:** two 20 W tubes can give very different amounts of light.
2. **Using 6500K everywhere:** it saves nothing and often feels harsh in bedrooms and living rooms.
3. **Retrofitting without checking the wiring:** a tube meant for a bypassed ballast, put into a circuit that still has the choke, can fail or overheat. A bypassed tube can also be live at both ends while it is being changed.

---

## Safety note

Always switch off at the MCB before changing or retrofitting a tube. Ballast bypass and fitting wiring should be done by a qualified electrician. Have a licensed electrician verify the installation before use.
