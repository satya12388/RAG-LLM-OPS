1. **Short-Circuit Protection:** In the event of a short circuit, fault currents can surge significantly, often reaching levels between 6,000A to 9,000A within milliseconds. A circuit breaker with a 10kA breaking capacity can safely interrupt these high fault currents without failing.

2. **Safety Assurance:** Lower-rated breakers (like 3kA or 4.5kA) may not have sufficient arc-splitting capabilities. When subjected to high fault currents, their contacts can melt and weld together, leading to a failure to trip. This can result in continuous fault current flow, posing a severe fire hazard.

3. **Compliance with Standards:** The 10kA rating is aligned with international safety standards, ensuring that electrical installations can handle potential fault conditions without endangering lives or property.

### Risks of Lower Icn Ratings:

1. **Inadequate Protection:** Circuit breakers with lower Icn ratings may not trip during high fault conditions, allowing dangerous currents to persist, which can lead to electrical fires or equipment damage.

2. **Increased Fire Hazard:** As mentioned, if the contacts of a lower-rated breaker weld together, it can lead to overheating and potentially ignite surrounding materials, resulting in a fire.

3. **Legal and Insurance Implications:** Using substandard equipment may violate local electrical codes and standards, potentially leading to legal issues or complications with insurance claims in the event of an electrical incident.

### Conclusion:

For residential safety, it is crucial to select circuit breakers with a minimum breaking capacity of 10kA as per IS/IEC 60898-1. This ensures that the electrical system can handle fault conditions effectively, minimizing risks to life and property. Always prioritize safety and compliance when choosing electrical products.
