Here’s a breakdown of why this specification is crucial and the risks associated with lower Icn ratings:

### Importance of 10kA Icn:

1. **Short-Circuit Protection:** In the event of a short circuit, fault currents can surge significantly, often reaching levels between 6,000A to 9,000A within milliseconds. A circuit breaker with a 10kA breaking capacity can safely interrupt these high fault currents without failure.

2. **Safety Assurance:** Lower-rated breakers (like 3kA or 4.5kA) may not have sufficient arc-splitting capabilities. When subjected to high fault currents, their contacts can melt and weld together, leading to continuous current flow. This can result in overheating, potential fires, and severe damage to the electrical system.

3. **Compliance with Standards:** The 10kA rating is aligned with safety standards that aim to protect both property and human life. Compliance with IS/IEC standards ensures that the electrical installations are robust and reliable.

### Risks of Lower Icn Ratings:

1. **Inadequate Fault Interruption:** Breakers with lower Icn ratings may not effectively interrupt fault currents, leading to prolonged exposure to dangerous conditions.

2. **Fire Hazards:** As mentioned, if the contacts of a lower-rated breaker weld together, it can lead to overheating and potentially cause electrical fires, endangering lives and property.

3. **Equipment Damage:** Continuous fault currents can damage not only the circuit breaker but also other connected electrical appliances and systems, leading to costly repairs and replacements.

4. **Legal and Insurance Issues:** Using substandard equipment may violate local electrical codes and standards, potentially leading to legal liabilities and issues with insurance coverage in the event of an electrical incident.

### Conclusion:

For residential safety, it is crucial to select circuit breakers with a minimum breaking capacity of 10kA as per IS/IEC 60898-1. This ensures that the electrical system can handle fault conditions safely, minimizing risks of fire and equipment damage. Always prioritize safety and compliance when choosing electrical products.

- Risks of lower Icn (e.g., 3kA or 4.5kA):
  - The breaker may fail to interrupt a real fault, allowing the arc to persist.
  - Contacts can weld shut, insulation can overheat, and a fire can initiate in the wall or distribution board.
  - In short, a lower Icn is inadequate for typical domestic fault levels and can compromise safety.
